Supplementary Figure 1: The growth of planktonic cells in the anolyte was monitored in two
MFC reactors, one fed with SWW (COD = 442 ± 8 mg mL-1, pH = 7.2 ± 0.1, Conductivity =
1.2 ± 0.1 mS cm-1) and then with real wastewater RWW (COD = 602 ± 7 mg mL-1, pH = 7.3 ±
0.1, Conductivity = 1.4 ± 0.1 mS cm-1). Cell proliferation was estimated using a) the OD600
method, and plate count of total aerobic heterotrophic culturable bacteria in b) Tryptic Soy
Agar (TSA), c) Nutrient Agar (NA), and d) Plate Count Agar (PCA). Samples of the anolyte
were taken at time 0 (after few minutes from anolyte replacement) and at various time intervals
over a period of two days, which corresponds to the maximum duration required to complete a
peak current production.




Supplementary Figure 2: Cyclic voltammograms of (a) MFC fed for over one month with
SWW and (b) MFC fed for over one month with RWW with the oxidation and reduction peaks
highlighted with yellow dots. The experiment was performed in the presence of oxidizable
substrate (TO conditions, dotted line), which was either SWW (COD = 442 mg mL-1, pH =
7.29, Conductivity = 1.31 mS cm-1) or RWW (COD = 169 mg mL-1, pH = 7.39, Conductivity
= 1.31 mS cm-1) and in absence of oxidizable substrate (NTO conditions, continuous line), with
Phosphate Buffer Saline solution (8 g L-1 NaCl; 0.2 g L-1 KCl; 1.44 g L-1 Na2HPO4; 0.14 g L-
1
   KH2PO4; pH=7.4).The analysis was performed using a PalmSense4 potentiostat connected
in a three-configuration mode (anode as WE, cathode CE, and Ag/AgCl RE) at 0.5 mV s-1.

annotated metagenome, expressed in Log2(Fold Change) at two levels of filtering a) Significant
differentially abundant KEGG annotations (adjusted p-value < 0.01) exceeding 300 genes per
million (GPM), and b) significant differentially abundant KEGG annotations (adjusted p-value
< 0.01) exhibiting the greatest Log2(Fold Change) upon transitioning from SWW to RWW.
Negative values indicate a decrease in gene abundance when moving from SWW to RWW,
whereas positive values signify an increase.
Supplementary Figure 5: Differential abundance of KEGG-annotated genes in anodic
biofilms subjected to RWW and SWW feeding. Values plotted on the positive side of the x-
axis represent an increased presence of genes under the RWW feeding. In contrast, annotations
on the negative x-axis highlight a decreased gene abundance, indicating these genes were more
prevalent in biofilms fed with SWW. The mean values are represented by coloured dots: a blue
dot indicates an overall increase in the specific annotation after biofilms were fed with RWW.
In contrast, a red dot signifies an overall decrease in the KEGG-annotated genes.
Supplementary Figure 6: Relative abundance of the lowest taxonomic rank assigned (species,
genus and families) of the inoculum used in the study. The inoculum used in this study was
sourced from an acetate-fed MFC, which had been in operation for more than a year and was
initially inoculated with sediment from the campus pond (University of Surrey, Guildford, UK).
Taxa with abundances exceeding 1% are individually displayed, while those with lower
abundances are collectively categorized as "other."




Supplementary Figure 7: Visual representation of the timeline of the MFC reactors,
showcasing the diverse interventions and analyses undertaken. These include replacement of
the GDE cathode, linear sweep voltammetry, cyclic voltammetry, taxonomic and
metagenomics analyses of the biofilm, and scanning electron microscopy of the anode and
cathode. The current study focuses on the last two phases of the experiment using artificial
wastewater and real wastewater as feeding solutions.
